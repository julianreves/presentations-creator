What it means

The deal lasted four days. The question it raised will last much longer: can Latin America's largest digital bank take its edge across borders without losing what made it work?

01 · The paradox

A Brazilian business with the customer base of a global bank.

Nubank's scaling problem starts with a contradiction. It already has the customer count of a global institution, yet its business remains geographically narrow.

Being enormous in one country is not the same as having a business that travels.

The gap is not all profit, since funding costs and credit losses come out of it. But it shows a bank built to serve huge populations cheaply. And on August 6, Nu began operating as a full commercial bank in Mexico, proof that the model can work beyond Brazil.

The mechanism behind the advantage is simple.

When customers receive their salary through Nubank…

…keep their savings there and pay their bills from the app…

…the bank sees their whole financial life, including people who have never had a credit file.

That picture makes every lending decision better and every new product more relevant. Then the loop starts again, with more data each time.

Anyone can copy the app. No one can copy what Nu knows about people with no credit history.
03 · The rival

Revolut built reach first while Nu built credit first.

Revolut took the opposite road to scale. At the end of 2025 it had 68.3 million retail customers, £4.5 billion in revenue and £1.7 billion in profit before tax.

Cards, subscriptions, foreign exchange, savings and wealth products make it a broad international proposition. Lending is the small part.

Each now wants what the other has. The risk for Nu is that someone contests its credit advantage at home. The opportunity is to connect international payments to the everyday banking relationship it already masters. Monzo looked like a shortcut into that race.

At £8 to 10 billion, Nu would have paid 46 to 58 times Monzo's adjusted profit before tax, before integration costs and execution risk. The deeper problem is not the price. Credit histories, consumer protections and payment systems differ in every country. Merging two brands and two technology stacks does not erase that.

In London, Nu's best skill would start from zero.
05 · The proposal

Follow the customer.

Nu's customers do not all stay home. Millions of them have family abroad, and the corridors they use already exist.

Home

Nu's three markets today: Brazil, Mexico and Colombia.

And London?

None of Nu's corridors leads to London. That was the Monzo door.

06 · The idea

Your credit history should move with you.

These migrants are the underbanked of rich countries. A Mexican who moves to Houston arrives with no US credit score. Nu may already hold years of that person's payment history.

We call this portable credit: lending from day one, using data the customer already generated at home. Revolut cannot copy it, and neither can a US bank.

Illustrative example. María is not a real customer. Portable credit does not exist yet; it is our proposal.

07 · The plan

Three moves, each built on something Nu already owns.

01

Become the sender's bank in the US.

Since January 2026, the US taxes cash-funded remittances at 1%. Transfers paid from a US bank account or card are exempt. That pushes senders from cash into accounts. In January, the OCC gave Nu conditional approval for a US national bank, which puts it in position to catch them.

1% excise tax on cash remittances, One Big Beautiful Bill Act, sec. 70604, effective Jan 1 2026. Tax only, provider fees excluded. OCC conditional approval: Jan 29 2026.
02

Make credit portable.

With the customer's consent, use home-country history to approve a card or loan in the new country. The hard part is legal: Brazil's data-protection law (LGPD) and US credit-reporting rules.

03

Enter Europe through Madrid and Lisbon, not London.

One EU license covers every member state, and the first customers would already speak Nu's languages and know its brand.

UKOutside the EU since 2020. A UK license does not passport into Europe.
08 · The risks

The risks we see:

01Smaller marketsA diaspora is smaller than a national market.
02A crowded businessRemittances already have Wise, Remitly and Western Union.
03Legal workMoving credit data across borders runs into privacy and credit-reporting law on both sides.
But each step builds on an asset Nu already owns. That is what makes a model repeatable.
The verdict

Monzo would have bought Nu a country, but... Following its customers would give them a model it can repeat.

The question for Nubank is not whether to look like Revolut. It is whether its next customers come from new countries or from communities it already understands.

Success should be measured in primary banking relationships and risk-adjusted returns, not in downloads or flags on a map.

Where should Nu's next 10 million customers come from?
